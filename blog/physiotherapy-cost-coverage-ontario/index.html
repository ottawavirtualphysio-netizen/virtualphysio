Do You Need a Referral for Physiotherapy in Ontario? Coverage, OHIP and Insurance Explained

Figuring out how to pay for physiotherapy can be confusing. Here's a plain-language guide to referrals, government funding and private insurance in Ontario.

By the Virtual Physio teamPublished November 25, 20263 min read

Do I need a doctor's referral?

No. In Ontario you can book directly with a registered physiotherapist without a referral from a doctor or nurse practitioner. Physiotherapists are trained to screen for problems that need medical attention and will refer you if needed.

Some private insurance plans still ask for a doctor's note before they reimburse physiotherapy, so check your plan's wording.

Does OHIP cover physiotherapy?

OHIP does not cover physiotherapy for most adults. However, the Ontario government funds physiotherapy for certain groups at designated community physiotherapy clinics, through home care, in long-term care homes and in some community health centres and family health teams.

For government-funded clinic physiotherapy, eligibility includes people with a valid OHIP card who are 65 or older, 19 or younger, or who were discharged from hospital after an overnight stay or outpatient surgery for the condition needing treatment, as well as people receiving Ontario Works or ODSP. Since April 1, 2024, a referral from a primary care provider is no longer required for these clinics. Check the current details on ontario.ca/page/get-physiotherapy.

Government-funded programs can have waitlists and set limits. Many people choose private physiotherapy for faster access, longer one-on-one sessions, or in-home and virtual options.

Extended health benefits (private insurance)

Most workplace and many individual health plans include physiotherapy. Before you book, check:

  • Annual maximum for physiotherapy, either a dollar amount or a number of visits
  • Coverage percentage, for example 80% of each visit
  • Per-visit limits, which may differ for the first assessment and follow-ups
  • Whether a doctor's note is required
  • Whether virtual and in-home visits are covered
  • Combined limits: some plans share one maximum across physiotherapy, massage and chiropractic

Common insurers in Ontario include Sun Life, Manulife, Canada Life, Blue Cross, Green Shield and Desjardins. Most let you check coverage in their app or member website.

Car accidents

If your injury happened in a car accident, physiotherapy may be funded through your own auto insurance accident benefits. See our guide to physiotherapy after a car accident in Ontario.

Workplace injuries

If you were injured at work, report it to your employer. Physiotherapy may be covered through WSIB (Workplace Safety and Insurance Board) if your claim is accepted.

Tax credits

Physiotherapy paid out of pocket may be claimable as a medical expense on your income tax return, as long as it's provided by a registered physiotherapist. Keep your receipts.

Tips for making the most of your coverage

  • Check your remaining balance before you book. Benefit years often reset in January.
  • Make sure your provider is a registered physiotherapist. Plans typically require this. You can check the College of Physiotherapists of Ontario public register.
  • Ask how many sessions are likely, so you can plan around your annual maximum.
  • Doing your home exercises consistently means you need fewer paid visits.
  • If you're covered by two plans, through your own and a spouse's, you may be able to coordinate benefits.

When you book with Virtual Physio, tell us your insurance provider and we'll help you understand what to ask your insurer.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a referral to see a physiotherapist in Ontario?

No. You can book directly with a registered physiotherapist. Some private insurance plans still require a doctor's note for reimbursement.

Is physiotherapy covered by OHIP?

Only for eligible groups, such as people 65 and older, 19 and under, those recently discharged from hospital or outpatient surgery, and Ontario Works or ODSP recipients, at government-funded clinics or through home care.

Is virtual physiotherapy covered by insurance?

Many extended health plans cover virtual physiotherapy by a registered physiotherapist, but coverage varies. Check with your insurer before booking.

This article is general information and isn't a substitute for an individual assessment. If you're unsure about your symptoms, speak to a physiotherapist or your doctor.

In-Home Physiotherapy in Ottawa: Who It Helps and What to Expect

For many people the hardest part of physiotherapy is getting to the appointment. In-home physiotherapy brings the assessment and treatment to you, and lets your plan fit the place where you actually live and move.

By the Virtual Physio teamPublished July 10, 2026 · Updated November 25, 20264 min read

What is in-home physiotherapy?

In-home physiotherapy is a one-on-one session with a physiotherapist in your own home rather than a clinic. The appointment includes the same core parts as a clinic visit: a conversation about your history and goals, a physical assessment, hands-on treatment where appropriate, exercise prescription and a clear plan for what to do between sessions.

The big difference is context. Your physiotherapist can see the stairs you climb, the chair you get up from, the bed you get in and out of and the space you have to exercise. That makes it much easier to build a plan you will actually follow.

Who benefits most from physiotherapy at home?

  • Older adults and people at risk of falls. Balance and strength training is most useful when it reflects your real environment: rugs, stairs, bathroom transfers and walking outdoors.
  • People recovering from surgery. After a hip or knee replacement, spinal surgery or a fracture, travelling to a clinic in the first weeks can be painful and tiring. Home visits let rehab start without the commute.
  • Anyone with limited transportation. If you don't drive, can't drive yet, or rely on family for rides, in-home care removes a major barrier.
  • People living with chronic pain or long-term conditions. Arthritis, persistent back pain or neurological conditions often mean energy is limited. Saving the trip leaves more energy for the session.
  • New parents. Postpartum and pelvic floor recovery can be easier to fit in when you don't need to arrange childcare or pack up a newborn.
  • Busy professionals. A home visit before or after work can be simpler than fitting a clinic trip into the day.

What happens at the first home visit?

Your first appointment usually takes about an hour. A typical visit looks like this:

  1. History and goals. What happened, what makes it better or worse, your medical history and medications, and what you want to get back to.
  2. Assessment. Movement, strength, flexibility, balance and walking. If relevant, your physiotherapist will also look at how you manage stairs, transfers and everyday tasks at home.
  3. Treatment. This may include hands-on techniques, guided exercise and education about your condition.
  4. A home program. A short list of exercises chosen for your space and equipment, with clear instructions on how often to do them.
  5. The plan. How many sessions are likely, what progress should look like and when to check in again.

How to prepare your home

You don't need special equipment. A little preparation helps the session run smoothly:

  • Clear a space about two metres by two metres, ideally near a sturdy chair and a wall or counter for support.
  • Wear comfortable clothing that lets the area being treated be seen and moved, such as shorts for a knee problem.
  • Have a list of medications, any surgical or imaging reports, and your insurance details nearby.
  • If you use a walker, cane or brace, keep it close so it can be checked.
  • Let us know about pets, parking or building access ahead of time.

Is in-home physiotherapy covered by insurance?

Many extended health benefit plans cover physiotherapy by a registered physiotherapist, and some cover home visits. Coverage varies by plan, so check your plan's physiotherapy limit and whether a doctor's note is required for reimbursement. If your care relates to a car accident, it may be covered through your auto insurance. See our guide to physiotherapy coverage in Ontario for details.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a doctor's referral for in-home physiotherapy in Ottawa?

No. In Ontario you can book directly with a physiotherapist. Some insurance plans ask for a doctor's note before they reimburse, so check your plan.

How long is an in-home physiotherapy session?

The first visit usually takes about 60 minutes. Follow-up visits are typically 30 to 45 minutes depending on your plan.

Do I need equipment at home?

No. Your physiotherapist will build exercises around what you have, such as a chair, a step or a wall. Resistance bands may be suggested later if helpful.

This article is general information and isn't a substitute for an individual assessment. If you're unsure about your symptoms, speak to a physiotherapist or your doctor.

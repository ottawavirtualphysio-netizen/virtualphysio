When to See a Physiotherapist After an Injury (and When to Go to the ER)

You twisted a knee, tweaked your back or fell on your shoulder. Should you wait it out, book physiotherapy or go to the emergency department? Here's a simple way to decide.

By the Virtual Physio teamPublished July 10, 2026 · Updated November 25, 20263 min read

First: does this need urgent medical care?

Go to the emergency department or call 911 if you have: an obvious deformity or a bone poking through the skin; you can't put any weight on the leg or use the arm; numbness, tingling or weakness that is spreading; a head injury with confusion, vomiting, worsening headache or loss of consciousness; new loss of bladder or bowel control or numbness in the groin; chest pain or shortness of breath; or a hot, red, swollen joint with fever.

If none of these apply, most sprains, strains and aches can be managed at home at first and assessed by a physiotherapist.

The first few days

For a new soft-tissue injury such as a sprained ankle or a pulled muscle:

  • Protect the area for one to three days by avoiding movements that sharply increase pain.
  • Elevate a swollen limb above heart level when resting.
  • Compress with a tensor bandage for swelling if comfortable.
  • Start moving gently as soon as pain allows. Gentle movement and walking usually help healing more than complete rest.
  • Ice can help relieve pain. Talk to a pharmacist or doctor about pain medication if needed.

Book a physiotherapist if:

  • Pain isn't clearly improving after 3 to 5 days
  • You're limping, can't climb stairs normally or can't lift your arm overhead
  • The joint feels unstable, gives way, locks or catches
  • Swelling or bruising is significant
  • You've injured the same area before
  • You need to get back to work, sport or caring for others safely and soon
  • Pain is affecting your sleep

In Ontario, you don't need a doctor's referral to see a physiotherapist. You can book directly. If the physiotherapist finds signs that you need imaging or a medical review, they will tell you and help you take the next step.

Why earlier is usually better

Early physiotherapy can help you recover faster, avoid the stiffness and weakness that come from protecting an injury for too long, and lower the risk of it happening again. Some injuries, such as ankle sprains, have a high re-injury rate when people return to activity without restoring balance and strength.

What happens at your first physiotherapy visit

  1. A conversation about how the injury happened and your symptoms since
  2. A physical exam of movement, strength, stability and nerve function
  3. A clear explanation of what's likely injured and how long recovery may take
  4. Treatment and a home program to start right away
  5. A plan for returning to work, sport and daily activities

Can an injury be assessed virtually?

Often, yes. A physiotherapist can guide you through movement and strength tests on video, give advice and start your exercise program, all without travel. If anything suggests you need hands-on testing or imaging, they'll let you know. If you're not sure which option suits you, send a request or call us and we'll talk it through.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a referral to see a physiotherapist in Ontario?

No. You can book directly with a registered physiotherapist. Some insurance plans require a doctor's note for reimbursement, so check your plan.

Should I use ice or heat after an injury?

Ice can help with pain in the first few days. Heat is often more comfortable later for stiffness. Use whichever eases your symptoms, and avoid putting either directly on bare skin.

How soon after an injury can I start physiotherapy?

Usually right away, once serious injuries have been ruled out. Early advice and gentle movement often lead to a faster recovery.

This article is general information and isn't a substitute for an individual assessment. If you're unsure about your symptoms, speak to a physiotherapist or your doctor.

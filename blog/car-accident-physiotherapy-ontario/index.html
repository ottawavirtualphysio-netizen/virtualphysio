Physiotherapy After a Car Accident in Ontario: Whiplash and Your Accident Benefits

After a car accident, pain and stiffness often show up a day or two later. Getting assessed early helps your recovery and makes the insurance process smoother.

By the Virtual Physio teamPublished November 25, 20263 min read
This article is general information, not legal or insurance advice. Talk to your insurer, and consider a licensed paralegal or lawyer, about your specific claim.

First steps after an accident

  1. Get medical help if you're injured. Go to the emergency department for head injuries, neck pain with numbness or weakness, chest or abdominal pain, or any severe pain.
  2. Report the collision as required and keep a copy of the details.
  3. Notify your own auto insurer as soon as possible. In Ontario, accident benefits come from your own insurance policy, regardless of who was at fault.
  4. Keep records of symptoms, appointments, receipts and time missed from work.

Common injuries after a motor vehicle accident

  • Whiplash: neck pain and stiffness from the rapid back-and-forth movement of the head
  • Upper and lower back strain
  • Shoulder injuries from the seatbelt or bracing on the steering wheel
  • Headaches, including those that start in the neck
  • Concussion symptoms such as dizziness, fogginess, light sensitivity and difficulty concentrating
  • Knee and hip injuries from hitting the dashboard
  • Sleep problems, anxiety and stress

Whiplash: what helps

Many people with whiplash improve over weeks to a few months, though recovery varies. Clinical guidance generally favours staying active, gentle neck movement and a graded exercise program over wearing a soft collar or resting for long periods. Physiotherapy may include:

  • Reassurance and clear education about expected recovery
  • Gentle range-of-motion exercises, progressing to strengthening
  • Hands-on techniques for short-term pain relief
  • Strategies for headaches, sleep and returning to driving and work
  • Vestibular and eye-movement exercises if you have dizziness

How accident benefits work in Ontario

Ontario's Statutory Accident Benefits Schedule (SABS) sets out the medical and rehabilitation benefits available through your auto insurance. A few key points:

  • Minor Injury Guideline (MIG). Injuries like sprains, strains and whiplash are usually treated under the MIG, which limits medical and rehabilitation benefits to $3,500 for those injuries.
  • Treatment plans. Your healthcare provider submits treatment plans to your insurer, which approves or denies them.
  • Outside the MIG. If your injuries are more serious, or a pre-existing condition will affect your recovery, you may be eligible for more funding. This is often decided through medical evidence and assessments.
  • Extended health benefits through work may also apply. They're often billed before or alongside auto benefits.

Why start physiotherapy early?

An early assessment documents your injuries and gives you a plan to stay active safely. It also helps make sure your treatment is planned within the funding that's available to you.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to pay for physiotherapy after a car accident in Ontario?

Treatment is often funded through your own auto insurance accident benefits and any extended health benefits. Contact your insurer to open a claim and confirm what's covered.

What is the Minor Injury Guideline?

It's a framework under Ontario's accident benefits rules for injuries like sprains, strains and whiplash. It limits medical and rehabilitation benefits for those injuries to $3,500.

How long does whiplash take to heal?

Recovery time varies. Many people improve over weeks to a few months. Your physiotherapist will set expectations after assessing you.

This article is general information and isn't a substitute for an individual assessment. If you're unsure about your symptoms, speak to a physiotherapist or your doctor.

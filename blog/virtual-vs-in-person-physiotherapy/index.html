Virtual vs In-Person Physiotherapy: Which Is Right for You?

Physiotherapy over video can sound surprising at first. But much of what helps people recover, such as assessment, education and a well-progressed exercise program, works just as well through a screen.

By the Virtual Physio teamPublished November 25, 20263 min read

How virtual physiotherapy works

A virtual appointment is a live, one-on-one video session with your physiotherapist using a phone, tablet or computer. It follows the same structure as an in-person visit:

  1. History. What happened, your symptoms, your health history and your goals.
  2. Assessment. Your physiotherapist guides you through movement, strength, balance and functional tests and watches how you move.
  3. Explanation. What's likely going on, in plain language, and what recovery should look like.
  4. Treatment. Exercises are taught and checked in real time, plus self-massage, pacing strategies and advice on your workstation or home set-up.
  5. Plan. A written home program and a schedule for follow-ups.

Does it actually work?

For many musculoskeletal conditions, research reviews have found that real-time telerehabilitation can lead to improvements in pain and function comparable to in-person care, particularly when treatment is centred on exercise and education (see references). Results vary by condition and person, and virtual care isn't right for everyone. Your physiotherapist will tell you if in-person care would suit you better.

Virtual physiotherapy is a good fit for

  • Back and neck pain, including desk-related pain
  • Exercise-based rehab after surgery once the early phase is over
  • Tendon problems, such as Achilles, patellar or rotator cuff tendinopathy
  • Knee and hip osteoarthritis
  • Pelvic floor education and training, and postpartum core recovery
  • Follow-up sessions to progress an existing program
  • Return-to-running and strength planning
  • People with busy schedules, limited transportation or who live outside central Ottawa

When in-person or in-home care is better

  • When hands-on treatment is a key part of the plan
  • When detailed hands-on testing is needed, for example to check ligament stability after a significant injury
  • For people at high risk of falls, who need someone physically present during balance work
  • When using video technology is difficult
  • The first days after surgery, when wound checks and walking aids need to be assessed

It isn't one or the other. Many people start with an in-home assessment and continue with virtual follow-ups, or the other way around.

How to get the most from a virtual session

  • Use a laptop or tablet if possible, propped up so your whole body can be seen when you step back.
  • Choose a well-lit room with a little floor space, and light coming from in front of you rather than behind.
  • Wear clothes that let the painful area be seen and moved.
  • Have a sturdy chair, a wall and, if you have them, a resistance band or light weights nearby.
  • Test your camera and internet a few minutes before the appointment.
  • Write down your questions in advance.

Is it private?

Virtual sessions are one-on-one, and your health information is kept confidential, just as it is in person. Choose a private room for the call if you can.

Frequently asked questions

Is virtual physiotherapy as effective as in-person?

For many conditions where exercise and education are the main treatment, research reviews suggest results can be comparable. Your physiotherapist will tell you if hands-on or in-person care would serve you better.

What do I need for a virtual physiotherapy appointment?

A phone, tablet or computer with a camera, a stable internet connection, a little floor space and comfortable clothing.

Is virtual physiotherapy covered by insurance?

Many extended health plans in Canada cover virtual physiotherapy by a registered physiotherapist, but check your plan's wording or call your insurer to confirm.
